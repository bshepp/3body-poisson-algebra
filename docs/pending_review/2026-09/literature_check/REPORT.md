# Pairwise three-body bracket counts remain unpublished

The per-bracket-length dimension sequence **3, 3, 8, 17, 44, 103, 272, 572** of the Lie algebra generated under the Poisson bracket by the three pairwise Hamiltonians H_ij = p_i²/2m_i + p_j²/2m_j + V(|q_i − q_j|) does not appear in the OEIS, in the splitting-integrator order-condition literature, in the N-body integrator literature, in the quantum Trotter-error literature, or in the dynamical-Lie-algebra/control literature, as of a full-text and search sweep on 2026-11-08. The OEIS query for the sequence returns verbatim "No results", and the only hit for the depth filtration 3, 6, 17, 116 is the author's own entry A395423 ([OEIS search](https://oeis.org/search?q=3,3,8,17,44,103,272,572&fmt=text); [OEIS search](https://oeis.org/search?q=3,6,17,116&fmt=text)). The closest existing work is McLachlan & Ryland (2003) and McLachlan & Murua (2019), who define "the Lie algebra of classical mechanics" L_P(A,B) — the free Lie algebra on two p-homogeneous generators (kinetic energy of degree 2, potential of degree 0) in the class of "polynomially graded" Lie algebras — and tabulate its order-graded dimensions 2, 1, 2, 2, 4, 5, 10, 14, 25, 39, 69, … with entropy 1.8249… ([arXiv:1905.07554](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.07554)). That object supplies the *mechanism* (identities forced by quadratic momentum dependence) and the *method* (generalized Witt counting of a quotient of a free Lie algebra), but not a comparable number: the three-body generators are three, each is a sum of a degree-2 and a degree-0 piece, and they carry a locality structure (disjoint-pair and same-body relations) that the two-generator theory has no room for. The honest framing is therefore that the three-body per-length sequence is a new, unreported instance of a well-named program — "universal Lie algebra generated by mechanical Hamiltonians, graded by bracket length, compared to the free (Witt) count" — and the main open task is to show whether its deficits from Witt's numbers (1, 4, 13, 40, 238 at lengths 4–8) are fully explained by class-P identities plus locality, which is a finite computation nobody has done.

Throughout, claims are marked as **[verbatim]** (stated in the source), **[inference]** (the researchers' or this writer's reading), or **[gap]** (not established either way).

## McLachlan–Murua's class-P algebra is the method, not the number

The comparison the reader most needs is precise, so the definitions come first. McLachlan & Murua's Definition 1 **[verbatim]**: "A Lie algebra L is of class P ('polynomially graded') if it is graded, i.e. L = ⊕_{n≥0} L_n, and its homogeneous subspaces L_n satisfy [L_n, L_m] ⊆ L_{n+m−1} if n > 0 or m > 0; and [L_0, L_0] = 0." Definition 2: "We denote as L_P(A,B) the free Lie algebra in the class P where A has degree 2 and B has degree 0, and we call it the Lie algebra of classical mechanics." The paper is explicit that this is a quotient by a *large* ideal, not by one relation: "L_P(A,B) = L(A,B)/I, where I is the Lie ideal generated by [L^0(A,B), L^0(A,B)]" ([arXiv:1905.07554 §2.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.07554)). The 2003 paper says the same in other words: "[BBBA] is not the only generator of the ideal ker ψ" ([arXiv:math-ph/0210030 §2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math-ph/0210030)). The structure theorem (Theorem 1) **[verbatim]**: "L_P(A,B) ≅ span(X) ⊕ L(A,[X,A]), where span(X) is abelian, of degree 0, and is isomorphic as a vector space to the free commutative nonassociative algebra generated by B under the operation U₁ ∗ U₂ := [U₂,[U₁,A]]", with one basis element per rooted full binary tree; the degree-0 counts 1, 1, 1, 2, 3, 6, 11, 23, 46, 98 at odd orders are the Wedderburn–Etherington numbers **[inference, confirmed against Table 5]**. Table 5 gives dim L^n_P(A,B) for n = 1..20 as **2, 1, 2, 2, 4, 5, 10, 14, 25, 39, 69, 110, 193, 320, 555, 938, 1630, 2786, 4852, 8370**, entropy 1/α = 1.8249111600523655937…, against the free two-generator entropy 2 ([arXiv:1905.07554 Table 5, §5](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1905.07554)). The 2003 values 194, 321, 557, 941, 1638, 2798, 4878, 8412 (n = 13–20) and entropy 1.82542377420108… are retracted: "their construction is in error starting at order 13" ([arXiv:1905.07554 §1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.07554); [arXiv:math-ph/0210030 Table 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math-ph/0210030)). Any citation should use the 2019 numbers. The Acta Numerica review's RKN column d_n = 2, 1, 2, 2, 4, 5, 10, 14, 25, 39, 69 is the same object for n ≤ 11, justified there by "[F₁,[F₁,[F₁,F₂]]] = 0 identically" plus "additional dependencies among nested commutators of C₁, C₂, C₃", e.g. "[C₃,[C₂,C₃]] vanishes identically" ([arXiv:2401.01722 §3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.01722)). An independent check in the research notes confirms that the single-relator quotient L(A,B)/⟨[B,[B,[B,A]]]⟩ has dimensions 2, 1, 2, 2, 4, 5, 10, **15, 26, 42** at degrees 1–10, diverging from L_P(A,B) at degree 8 (15 vs 14), which demonstrates concretely that the mechanical algebra is *smaller* than "free modulo the length-4 identity" **[inference; script `rkn_quotient_dims.py` in the notes folder]**. This matters for the three-body case: finding one relation at length 4 does not predict the later shortfalls, and the two-generator precedent says exactly that.

Now the differences, stated sharply. First, **generator count and homogeneity**. L_P(A,B) has two generators, each homogeneous in momentum degree, which is what makes "degree" a grading and "order" (bracket length, order(A) = order(B) = 1) a second grading. Each pairwise Hamiltonian H_ij = T_i + T_j + V_ij is a *sum* of a degree-2 and a degree-0 piece, so the three-body algebra is not graded by degree at all, only filtered; the class-P quotient L(A,B)/⟨[L⁰,L⁰]⟩ cannot be applied to the H_ij as abstract generators **[inference]**. McLachlan & Ryland's two easy multi-generator cases do not help either: "the Lie algebra with k generators of degree ≥ 1 which is free in the class P is just the standard free Lie algebra on k generators", and the degree-0/degree-1 mixed case "only contains elements of degree 0 and 1" ([arXiv:math-ph/0210030 §1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math-ph/0210030)); the three-body generators are in neither class. Second, **locality**. The natural enlarged generating set {T₁, T₂, T₃, V₁₂, V₁₃, V₂₃} *is* p-homogeneous (degrees 2, 2, 2, 0, 0, 0), so the class-P framework applies to it, but the three-body algebra satisfies relations beyond class P that are invisible to any two-generator theory: {T_i, T_j} = 0, {V_ij, V_kl} = 0 (already class P), and crucially {T_k, V_ij} = 0 whenever k ∉ {i, j} and {H_ij, H_kl} = 0 for disjoint pairs — which for N = 3 never happens, since any two of the three pairs share a body **[inference]**. The author's own variant experiments at bracket length 4 are consistent with exactly this reading: the length-4 relation survives curved or shifted quadratic kinetic terms, external traps and non-translation-invariant potentials, but disappears when "three random flat quadratic kinetic forms + random potentials (no body structure)" are used, when "V₁₂ also depends on body 3", when potentials contain momentum, or when kinetic terms have p³ or p⁴ pieces; the recorded conjecture is "that the relation is this identity [{V_c,{V_a,{V_b,T}}} = 0], seen through the free Lie algebra and filtered by locality. Not yet derived by hand" ([REVIEW_PENDING.md B7](/home/user/3body-poisson-algebra/docs/pending_review/2026-09/REVIEW_PENDING.md)). That conjecture is the correct bridge to McLachlan–Murua and should be stated as such.

Can the three-body per-length numbers be derived from or bounded by their framework? Three statements can be made with confidence **[inference]**. (i) The free Lie algebra on three generators is an exact upper bound at every length (Witt), and the three-body algebra is a Lie subalgebra, generated by the three sums T_i + T_j + V_ij, of the realization of the free class-P algebra on the six homogeneous generators; a bracket of length n in the H_ij expands into order-n brackets of the six, so the length-n piece of the three-body algebra is bounded above by the order-n piece of L_P(T₁,T₂,T₃,V₁₂,V₁₃,V₂₃)/⟨locality⟩ restricted to the image of that subalgebra. (ii) Nothing in the two papers computes or bounds this six-generator class-P algebra, let alone the locality quotient; the generalized Witt formula they use, "dim Lⁿ(W) = Σ_{d|n} (1/d) μ(d) [t^{n/d}](−log(1 − w(t)))", applies only to a *free* algebra on a graded set W, which they obtain for L_P(A,B) through Lazard elimination; the analogous elimination for six generators with locality has not been done ([arXiv:1905.07554 §2.2, Eq. 10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.07554)). (iii) Whether the physical three-body algebra equals the "universal" one (free in class P with locality, on three pairwise sums) is the exact analogue of McLachlan–Murua's Conjecture 1, "there are no relations other than those inherited from L_P(A,B) … that are shared by all possible smooth Euclidean mechanical systems with arbitrary dimensions" ([arXiv:1905.07554 §3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.07554)). The author's reported potential-, dimension- and mass-invariance of 3, 6, 17, 116 is evidence for the three-body analogue holding; the counter-examples r¹, r², r³ (sequences [3,4,5,5], [3,6,13,15], [3,6,17,109]) are evidence that specific potentials can add relations, exactly as cubic potentials shrink the two-generator counts in McLachlan–Ryland's Table 2 "Cubic" column (2, 1, 2, 2, 3, 3, 6, 6, 10, 12, 19, 22) ([README](/home/user/3body-poisson-algebra/README.md); [arXiv:math-ph/0210030 Table 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math-ph/0210030)). **[gap]**: no one has computed the universal three-body object, so it is not known whether 17, 44, 103, 272, 572 are "universal" counts or whether the physical algebra for 1/r already sits strictly below the universal one at some length ≤ 8. The author's own note that 1D and planar block ranks "agree at every word length ≤ 8 and split at lengths 9–12 (e.g. 550 vs 609 at length 9)" shows the physical algebra depends on spatial dimension at length 9, which the universal object cannot, so the universal counts must be ≥ 609 at length 9 **[inference from REVIEW_PENDING.md B4]**.

One more framing point. McLachlan–Murua's "order" grading is the right analogue of the per-length grading, and their Table 3 bigrading (order × degree) has no three-body counterpart because the H_ij are inhomogeneous. The depth filtration A_n = A_{n−1} + {A_{n−1}, A_{n−1}} of A395423 has no counterpart in either McLachlan paper or the splitting literature at all; depth-n brackets reach length 2ⁿ, and the depth filtration is coarser information than the length grading (the author's records give the free depth-3 count as 119 and the physical as 116, with the whole difference attributed to the single length-4 relation; see [REVIEW_PENDING.md B2](/home/user/3body-poisson-algebra/docs/pending_review/2026-09/REVIEW_PENDING.md)) **[author's claim, unreviewed]**. For connecting to the integrator literature, the per-length sequence is the object to lead with, since "number of independent order conditions at order n" is a per-length count.

## N-body integrator papers classify vanishing brackets but never count them

The three-body pairwise generators are precisely the per-pair Kepler Hamiltonians of the Hernandez–Bertschinger and SAKURA splittings. Hernandez & Bertschinger write **[verbatim]** "H = T + V = T + ΣΣ_{i,j>i} V_ij = T + ΣΣ_{i,j>i} (K_ij − T_ij). Here … T_ij is the kinetic energy of particle i plus particle j, and K_ij is the two body Hamiltonian for particles i and j (K stands for Kepler) including both relative and center of mass terms" ([arXiv:1503.02728 §2.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.02728)), and SAKURA uses H_{K_ij} = μ_ij(½v_ij² − (m_i+m_j)/r_ij) with H_{W_ij} = H_{K_ij} − H_{T_ij} ([arXiv:1402.3325 §2.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.3325)). So the author's algebra is the Lie algebra of error terms of a pairwise-Kepler splitting of the three-body problem, up to the bookkeeping that Σ_{i<j} H_ij = 2T + V for N = 3, which the integrators absorb by subtracting pair kinetic energies or (equivalently for the algebra, given the reported mass invariance) by rescaling the kinetic coefficients **[inference]**. That is the strongest "what is it an instance of" statement available: the per-length dimensions are the numbers of independent order conditions a general (non-symmetric) composition of the three pair flows exp(hL_{H_12}), exp(hL_{H_13}), exp(hL_{H_23}) must satisfy at each order, in exactly the sense Laskar–Vaillant and Blanes–Casas–Murua use ("the number of such independent conditions arising at each order n can be obtained just by determining the dimension of L_n(F₁,F₂), the linear span of all commutators containing n operators", [arXiv:2401.01722 §2.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.01722)).

What the integrator papers actually do is classify non-vanishing bracket *types* by particle content, never dimensions. Dehnen & Hernandez state **[verbatim]**: "The second contribution to the errors in equations (12) are sums over terms of the form {{T, V_ij}, V_lk}. These vanish if all four indices differ, and the only non-vanishing terms are of two types: {{T,V_ij},V_ij} = G²m_i m_j(m_i+m_j)/r_ij⁴ (14a) and {{T,V_ij},V_ik} = G²m_i m_j m_k (x_ij·x_ik)/(r_ij³ r_ik³) with j ≠ k (14b)", they decompose {{T,V},V} = {{T,V},V}₂ + {{T,V},V}₃ by number of distinct bodies, and in Appendix B1 they note "the nested Poisson brackets {TV⁴} and {TV³T} vanish (regardless of their coefficients)" and split the surviving order-h⁴ terms into 2-, 3- and 4-body contributions ([arXiv:1609.09375 §2.2, App. A1, B1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.09375)). They also record the strongest structural statement in that literature: "error terms which are (nested) Poisson brackets containing only T and V_ij are eliminated at all orders … the only remaining error terms involve two or more particle pairs" ([arXiv:1609.09375 §3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.09375)). Hernandez & Dehnen's planetary paper gives the single exhaustive enumeration found: for the four-piece split {T⁰, T¹, V⊙, V_pl}, "All possible O(h²) terms which may occur in the surrogate Hamiltonian … are" the eight brackets (41a)–(41h), followed by "All other independent nested Poisson brackets of these basic building blocks vanish"; they also list the six O(h⁴) bracket types for the two-piece WHD split, which equals the free two-generator count at degree 5 but is not stated as a count ([arXiv:1612.05329 §4.1.1–4.1.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.05329)). Hernandez & Bertschinger derive via BCH that the O(h) term "vanishes exactly" and that the O(h²) surrogate "has no dependence on the order of pairs in the integrator to second order" ([arXiv:1503.02728 §2.2.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.02728)). Blanes et al. 2013 go furthest toward a three-generator count, but only for the near-integrable two-piece problem recast with three elimination variables: "(10,6,4) methods require, in addition to consistency, 22 order conditions" ([arXiv:1208.0689 §2.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.0689)). **[gap]**: full-text searches of arXiv:1503.02728, 1402.3325, 1609.09375, 1511.07476, 1612.05329 and 2401.07113 for "number of", "independent", "free Lie", "dimension" return nothing beyond the sentences quoted; SAKURA's paper contains no bracket analysis at all. No paper phrases the pairwise pieces as generating a Lie algebra "free modulo locality and p-structure", and none tabulates a dimension.

The quantum Trotter-error literature is the mirror image: it uses locality systematically but counts norms, not dimensions. Childs, Su, Tran, Wiebe & Zhu's Theorem 11 defines **[verbatim]** "α̃_comm = Σ_{γ₁,…,γ_{p+1}=1}^Γ ‖[H_{γ_{p+1}}, ··· [H_{γ₂}, H_{γ₁}]]‖" and bounds the additive and multiplicative Trotter errors by O(α̃_comm t^{p+1}) ([arXiv:1912.08854 §3.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.08854)). For k-local Hamiltonians they argue exactly as Dehnen–Hernandez do: "In order for this commutator to be nonzero, every operator must have support that overlaps with the support of operators from the inner layers. Using this idea, we estimate that α̃_comm = O(|||H|||₁^p ‖H‖₁)" ([arXiv:1912.08854 §4.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.08854)). But the sum runs over all Γ^{p+1} index tuples with repetitions, no antisymmetry or Jacobi reduction, and the full text contains no occurrence of "free Lie", "Witt", "Lyndon", "Hall basis" or "linearly independent" **[verified by grep of the pdftotext output]**. They also warn that "this theorem does not follow from truncating the BCH series" because for fixed t a higher-order term can dominate ([arXiv:1912.08854 §1.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.08854)), which is a useful caution for anyone wanting to read the per-length dimensions as error magnitudes. The structural difference from the classical case is that in the N-body split the kinetic energy T is global, so overlap with T is automatic and only the pairwise-Kepler form K_ij (kinetic terms restricted to the pair) makes disjoint-pair brackets truly vanish **[inference]**; for N = 3 there are no disjoint pairs, so locality enters only through the same-body sharing structure.

## Dynamical Lie algebras and growth vectors are analogues, not precedents

Two named concepts have the same *construction* and should be cited as analogues only. In variational quantum circuits, the dynamical Lie algebra (DLA) is "the real span of the Lie closure (i.e., closure under taking commutators) of the generators" ([Diaz et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.11505)); Larocca et al. tie barren plateaus to "the scaling of the dimension of g" ([arXiv:2105.14377](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14377v3)), Wiersema et al. "find 17 unique dynamical Lie algebras" for translation-invariant 2-local chains ([arXiv:2309.05690](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2309.05690)), and Kökcü et al. classify DLAs of spin interactions on graphs, finding that outside one dimension "the dynamical Lie algebra solely depends on whether the graph is bipartite or not" ([arXiv:2409.19797](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.19797)). The interaction-graph organisation is the nearest quantum cousin of "pairwise Hamiltonians on N bodies", but every accessible DLA paper reports only the dimension of the *closed* algebra, which for Pauli strings is finite (≤ 4ⁿ − 1); the depth filtration appears only as the iteration counter of closure algorithms such as PennyLane's `lie_closure` ("max_iterations (int): maximum depth of nested commutators to consider") ([PennyLane docs](https://docs.pennylane.ai/en/stable/code/api/pennylane.lie_closure.html)). In classical geometric control the "growth vector" of a bracket-generating distribution is by definition a dimension sequence indexed by bracket depth ("The dimensions of these successive spaces form the growth vector", [arXiv:2401.07560](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.07560)), with free nilpotent models realising the Witt-type maxima (cumulative 2, 3, 5, 8, 14, 23, 41, 71 for two generators, [arXiv:1304.1035](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1304.1035)); but it is pointwise and bounded by the manifold dimension, whereas the three-body function algebra is infinite-dimensional. Sarychev's chain-of-particles controllability papers use pairwise potentials and Lie-rank arguments but switch external forces, not interactions, and give no dimension sequences ([arXiv:0809.2365](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0809.2365)). Dullin's reduced N-body Lie–Poisson structure sp(2n−2) is the closure of *quadratic* invariants, matching the author's harmonic closure at dimension 15 in spirit, and is not an iterated-bracket filtration ([arXiv:1207.5883](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.5883)) **[inference]**. **[gap]**: no theorem on the Gelfand–Kirillov dimension or growth of finitely generated Lie subalgebras of rational-function Poisson algebras was found; the only adjacent results are Bavula's bound for isotropic subalgebras and Petrogradsky's growth hierarchy ([arXiv:math/0401263](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0401263)).

5. **Compute the universal three-body object.** Build the free Lie algebra on six graded generators T₁, T₂, T₃ (degree 2) and V₁₂, V₁₃, V₂₃ (degree 0), quotient by the class-P ideal ⟨[L⁰, L⁰]⟩ and the locality ideal ⟨[T_i, T_j], [T_k, V_ij] for k ∉ {i,j}⟩, form the three sums X_ij = T_i + T_j + V_ij, and compute the rank of the span of length-n brackets of the X_ij in the order-n piece for n ≤ 8 (and ideally 9–10). If the result is 3, 3, 8, 17, 44, 103, 272, 572 the sequence is "universal", fully explained by quadratic-momentum identities plus locality, and the correct title for the object is the three-body pairwise analogue of L_P(A,B); if it is strictly larger at some n, the physical algebra carries genuinely extra identities and the length at which they first appear is itself a result. Either outcome is publishable and either settles the novelty question precisely. The author's 1D-versus-planar split at length 9 implies the universal count there is at least 609.
6. **Derive the length-4 relation by hand** from {V_c, {V_a, {V_b, T}}} = 0 restricted to the three sums, as the conjecture in REVIEW_PENDING.md B7 proposes, and check whether the length-5 deficit of 4 and length-6 deficit of 13 are generated by it (ad-images of the length-4 relation) or require new relations — the two-generator precedent (single-relator quotient 15 vs L_P 14 at degree 8) predicts new relations will be needed eventually.
7. **Report an entropy estimate only with a caveat.** The per-length ratios 572/272 ≈ 2.10 and 272/103 ≈ 2.64 have not converged; do not quote a growth constant as the analogue of 1.8249… until more terms exist or the universal object's generating function is known.
8. **Compute the bigraded refinement** by momentum degree (a filtration, since the generators are inhomogeneous) to produce the analogue of McLachlan–Murua's Table 3, which is the most direct way a reader of their paper will recognise the structure.

**Statement of novelty.** The per-bracket-length dimension sequence 3, 3, 8, 17, 44, 103, 272, 572 and the depth sequence 3, 6, 17, 116 are, on the evidence of full-text reads of the primary sources and exhaustive OEIS and web searches on 2026-11-08, unpublished anywhere other than the author's own A395423, repository and Zenodo deposit. They are not, however, a new kind of object: they are the three-generator, pairwise-local instance of the program McLachlan & Ryland opened in 2003 and McLachlan & Murua corrected in 2019 — compute the universal Lie algebra generated by mechanical Hamiltonians, grade it by bracket length, and measure its deficit from the free Lie algebra — applied to the pieces of the pairwise-Kepler splittings of Hernandez–Bertschinger and SAKURA, whose authors classified but never counted the resulting brackets. What is new is the generating set and the numbers; what is not yet established is whether the numbers are the universal (potential-independent) counts of that algebra or already carry extra identities of the 1/r three-body problem, and the single most valuable next step is the finite computation in item 5 that decides this.

## Conclusion

The literature check changes the question the author should be asking. "Is the sequence known?" has a clean negative answer, but it is the less interesting question; the two-generator precedent shows that the interesting invariant is not the raw dimension sequence but the ideal of identities behind it, and that this ideal is larger than its lowest-order generator (L_P(A,B) falls below the one-relator quotient at degree 8, and the three-body shortfalls 1, 4, 13, 40, 238 already resist a closed form). McLachlan and Murua turned their counts into a theorem — abelian modified potentials plus a free part, with a computable entropy — by finding the right elimination; the three-body object will become a theorem rather than a table when the universal six-generator class-P algebra with locality is constructed and the three pairwise sums are tracked inside it.

That construction also resolves the framing tension between the two audiences this work can reach. For numerical analysts the per-length numbers are order-condition counts for a three-piece Kepler splitting, and the correct claim is that they refine Dehnen–Hernandez's "two or more particle pairs" bookkeeping into exact dimensions; for the quantum and control communities the depth filtration is a growth vector of an infinite-dimensional dynamical Lie algebra, a quantity those fields define but never tabulate because their algebras close. Both framings are honest, both rest on the same unperformed computation, and neither requires the sequence to match anything already in print.
