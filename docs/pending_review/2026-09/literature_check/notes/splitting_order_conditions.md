## Key question 1: The standard statement that order conditions are indexed by a basis of the free Lie algebra, and who gives per-order counts

### Takeaway
It is standard (Blanes–Casas–Murua 2008/2024, Auzinger et al. 2016, Casas & Escorihuela-Tomàs 2020, McLachlan & Quispel 2002 §4.3) that the number of independent order conditions at order n for a splitting into k pieces equals the dimension of the degree-n homogeneous component of the free Lie algebra on k generators, given by Witt's formula; for two generators that is 2, 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18, 30, 56, 99, … and for three generators 3, 3, 8, 18, 48, 116, 312, 810, … (OEIS A027376). For time-symmetric methods only odd-degree conditions survive.

### Cited Findings
- Auzinger, Hofstätter, Koch, Thalhammer, "The BCH-formula and order conditions for splitting methods" (arXiv 1604.01190): "the number of such independent conditions arising at each order n can be obtained just by determining the dimension of the linear span of all commutators containing n operators" — [arXiv 1604.01190](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.01190) (via search snippet).
- Casas & Escorihuela-Tomàs, "Composition methods for dynamical systems separable into three parts" (Mathematics 8(4) 533, 2020; arXiv 2006.06317): "the specific number of order conditions is determined by the dimension c_k of the homogeneous subspace of grade k of the free Lie algebra L(A, B, C)"; their table of per-grade counts for three operators was reported by the search engine as "3 3 8 18 45 116 312 810" for grades 1–8, and the paper states that a (non-symmetric) method of order 4 requires solving 3+3+8+18 = 32 conditions while a symmetric method of order 4 requires 11 (= 3 + 8) — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7390/8/4/533); [arXiv 2006.06317](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.06317). **Note:** the "45" at grade 5 in the snippet contradicts Witt's formula ((3^5 − 3)/5 = 48) and the rest of the row (which is exactly A027376); I regard it as a transcription error in the snippet, not in the paper, but could not verify.
- OEIS A027376: "Number of ternary irreducible monic polynomials of degree n; dimensions of free Lie algebras", a(n) = (1/n) Σ_{d|n} μ(d) 3^{n/d}, values 1, 3, 3, 8, 18, 48, 116, 312, 810, … from n = 0; also the number of Lyndon words of length n over a 3-letter alphabet — [OEIS A027376](https://oeis.org/A027376).
- Witt's formula M(α, n) = (1/n) Σ_{d|n} μ(d) α^{n/d} gives "the dimension of the degree n piece of the free Lie algebra on α generators", equivalently the number of aperiodic necklaces / Lyndon words — [Blessenohl & Laue, Séminaire Lotharingien de Combinatoire](https://www.mat.univie.ac.at/~slc/opapers/s29laue.pdf); [Cambridge, "On Witt's dimension formula…"](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/bulletin-of-the-australian-mathematical-society/article/on-witts-dimension-formula-for-free-lie-algebras-and-a-theorem-of-klyachko/1E9387A845BAA2C3973E05F0D26539CB).

### Inferences
- Two-generator Witt dimensions (my computation, matches the standard formula): n = 1..12 → 2, 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18, 30, 56, 99, 186, 335 (OEIS A001037). Cumulative non-symmetric counts for a general two-piece splitting: order 1: 2, 2: 3, 3: 5, 4: 8, 5: 14, 6: 23, 7: 41, 8: 71, 9: 127, 10: 226. Symmetric (odd degrees only): order 2: 2, 4: 4, 6: 10, 8: 28, 10: 84. The "28 at order 8" and "10 at order 6" agree with the figures quoted from Blanes–Casas–Escorihuela-Tomàs 2022 (see KQ2), confirming that their "general splitting" column is exactly the Witt sequence.
- Three-generator Witt dimensions (my computation): 3, 3, 8, 18, 48, 116, 312, 810, 2184, 5880, 16104, 44220 — identical to OEIS A027376, so the "45" in the Casas–Escorihuela-Tomàs snippet should be read as 48.
- The "2 at order 1" convention counts the consistency conditions Σa_i = 1, Σb_i = 1 as order-1 conditions; some authors omit these from totals, which explains minor discrepancies between tables (e.g. "3 order conditions" vs "4 conditions" for sixth-order symmetric compositions of a symmetric second-order method, see [arXiv 2101.04100](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.04100) snippet).

### Gaps

## Key question 2: Exact per-order counts in the RKN / separable case and how they are derived

### Takeaway
In the separable case (A = kinetic, quadratic in p; B = potential, p-free) the single degree-4 identity [B,[B,[B,A]]] = 0 (written [F_b,[F_b,[F_a,F_b]]] ≡ 0 or F_{1112} = 0 by Blanes–Casas et al.) generates an ideal, and the RKN order conditions are indexed by a basis of the quotient Lie algebra. The literature (Blanes–Casas–Escorihuela-Tomàs 2022, Table 1; BCM 2008 Table 2; BCM 2024 Table 2.1) states that symmetric RKN methods need 8 (not 10) conditions at order 6 and 18 (not 28) at order 8. My independent computation of the quotient dimensions gives per-degree 2, 1, 2, 2, 4, 5, 10, 15, 26, 42 for degrees 1–10, reproducing exactly those two published totals.

### Cited Findings
- Blanes, Casas, Escorihuela-Tomàs, "Runge–Kutta–Nyström symplectic splitting methods of order 8", Appl. Numer. Math. 182 (2022) 14–27 (arXiv 2202.01541): "Table 1 gives the number of independent order conditions for compositions of symmetric second-order methods, in both the general splitting case and the RKN case, up to order 10"; "[F_b,[F_b,[F_a,F_b]]] ≡ 0, and many terms in (2.3) vanish identically, so that their order conditions can be ignored"; reducing "up to order 6 saves only two equations relative to general splitting methods, but a time-symmetric method of order 8 requires solving 18 order conditions instead of 28"; "the minimum number of stages for a RKN splitting method of order 8 is s = 17" — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0168927422001842); [UJI repository PDF](https://repositori.uji.es/server/api/core/bitstreams/be9da140-c382-4a1f-888f-7be546c4d208/content); [arXiv 2202.01541](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.01541).
- Blanes, Casas, Murua 2024 (Acta Numerica): "F_{1112} = 0 in (2.21), which introduces linear dependences among higher order terms in the expansion of log(Ψ(h)) for RKN-type splitting, which reduces the number of order conditions"; "the actual number d_r of order conditions for orders r ≤ 11 are given in Table 2.1" — [arXiv 2401.01722](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.01722).

### Inferences
- **Derivation.** For H = T(p) + V(q) with T quadratic, {T,V} is linear in p, {{T,V},V} is p-free, hence {{{T,V},V},V} = 0 identically; in Lie-derivative language with A = L_T, B = L_V this is [B,[B,[B,A]]] = 0 (equivalently [F_b,[F_b,[F_a,F_b]]] = 0). Which letter carries the relation depends purely on the authors' labelling convention (A↔kinetic in BCM; "operator 1" in the 2024 review's F_{1112}); in the ordering used in the task statement ("[A,[A,[A,B]]]=0 depending on convention") it is the same single relation.
- **Quotient algebra.** The ideal I generated by R = [B,[B,[B,A]]] in L(A,B) is spanned in degree n by the 2^{n−4} elements ad_{x_1}⋯ad_{x_{n−4}} R, x_i ∈ {A,B} (since ad_{[u,v]} = [ad_u, ad_v], no other brackets are needed). I computed dim I_n and dim L_n/I_n exactly over Q in the free associative algebra (script saved alongside these notes as `rkn_quotient_dims.py`; runtime ~25 s to degree 10):

  | degree n | dim L_n (general, Witt) | dim I_n | dim (L/I)_n (RKN) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | 2 | 0 | 2 |
  | 2 | 1 | 0 | 1 |
  | 3 | 2 | 0 | 2 |
  | 4 | 3 | 1 | 2 |
  | 5 | 6 | 2 | 4 |
  | 6 | 9 | 4 | 5 |
  | 7 | 18 | 8 | 10 |
  | 8 | 30 | 15 | 15 |
  | 9 | 56 | 30 | 26 |
  | 10 | 99 | 57 | 42 |

  Symmetric (odd-degree) cumulative totals: order 4: 4 (= general), order 6: 8 vs 10, order 8: 18 vs 28, order 10: 44 vs 84. Non-symmetric cumulative: order 4: 7 vs 8, 5: 11 vs 14, 6: 16 vs 23, 7: 26 vs 41, 8: 41 vs 71, 9: 67 vs 127, 10: 109 vs 226. The two numbers the snippets give (8 instead of 10 at order 6; 18 instead of 28 at order 8) are reproduced exactly, so I am confident these are the l_r of BCM 2008 Table 2 / Table 1 of the 2022 paper, but the published tables themselves were not read. Note dim I_n = 2^{n−4} for n = 4..7 (all ad-images independent) and drops below it from n = 8 (15 < 16), so the quotient is *not* simply "free minus 2^{n−4}".
- Whether the Lie algebra actually generated by L_T and L_V for a generic separable Hamiltonian is exactly this one-relator quotient (and not smaller, i.e. no further identities in higher degree) is implicitly assumed in the splitting literature when they say the RKN conditions are l_r in number; I found no published proof statement, but the agreement of the counts is consistent with it.

## Key question 3: Multi-piece (three or more) splittings, including N-body pairwise/Kepler splittings — has anyone counted conditions or the dimension of the generated Lie algebra?

### Takeaway
Generic three-piece splittings have been treated with free-Lie-algebra counting (Casas & Escorihuela-Tomàs 2020: 3, 3, 8, 18, … per grade; 32 conditions for order 4, 11 if symmetric). The astrophysical pairwise/Kepler splitting papers (Hernandez & Bertschinger 2015, Dehnen & Hernandez 2017, Wisdom–Holman family, SyMBA, Mercury) work with nested Poisson brackets of their pieces but, as far as the accessible material shows, none counts order conditions or the dimension of the Lie algebra generated by the pieces.

### Cited Findings
- Casas & Escorihuela-Tomàs 2020: order conditions for splitting into three parts A, B, C are indexed by the free Lie algebra L(A,B,C); a 4th-order method needs 32 conditions, a symmetric one 11 — [arXiv 2006.06317](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.06317); [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7390/8/4/533).
- Dehnen & Hernandez, "Symplectic fourth-order maps for the collisional N-body problem", MNRAS 465 (2017) 1201–1217 (arXiv 1609.09375): analyses "symplectic, time-reversible N-body integrators that use a Kepler solver for each pairwise interaction, including the Hernandez & Bertschinger method"; uses operator splitting and the BCH formula, expressing the surrogate Hamiltonian "as a series of nested Poisson brackets" (e.g. {{B,A},A}); finds "close two-body encounters are handled correctly, while close three-body encounters contribute to truncation error at second order and above"; the second-order errors can be corrected to give a fourth-order scheme; also generalizes so a Kepler solver is used only for selected interactions — [ADS](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.465.1201D); [arXiv 1609.09375](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.09375); [Leicester figshare](https://figshare.le.ac.uk/articles/journal_contribution/Symplectic_fourth-order_maps_for_the_collisional_N-body_problem/10232159). No snippet mentions counting error terms, order conditions, or a Lie-algebra dimension.
- Hernandez & Bertschinger, "Symplectic integration for the collisional gravitational N-body problem", MNRAS 2015 (arXiv 1503.02728): reversible symplectic integrator using Kepler solvers, conserving nine N-body integrals to machine precision, "second order but can be raised to higher order with Yoshida's method" — [arXiv 1503.02728](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.02728v2); [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/108694?show=full). No order-condition count mentioned.
- Hernandez's MIT PhD thesis compares different Hamiltonian splittings through backward error analysis — [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/119107).
- Gonçalves Ferrari, Boekholt & Portegies Zwart, "Keplerian-based Hamiltonian splitting for gravitational N-body simulations", MNRAS 440 (2014) 719: approximates the N-body problem "as a composition of independently evolved two-body problems"; the composition "is exact at the level of the Hamiltonian split but yields a non-symplectic, non-time-symmetric first-order map when the independent two-body flows are composed" — [OUP MNRAS](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/440/1/719/1747624).
- Blanes, Casas, Farrés, Laskar, Makazaga, Murua, "New families of symplectic splitting methods for numerical integration in dynamical astronomy", Appl. Numer. Math. 2013 (arXiv 1208.0689): derives "an independent set of necessary and sufficient order conditions" for near-integrable Hamiltonians H = H_A + εH_B (generalized order (r_1, r_2)), and shows Poincaré heliocentric coordinates need one extra polynomial equation — [arXiv 1208.0689](https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.0689).
- BCM 2024 classify the Wisdom–Holman map (Kepler motions + planetary interactions, Kepler part solved exactly) as a scheme of generalized order (2,2) — [arXiv 2401.01722](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.01722).
- Rein, Tamayo & Brown implemented several high-order symplectic (Wisdom–Holman-type) methods in REBOUND — [arXiv 1907.11335](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1907.11335); no condition counting in snippets.

### Inferences
- The generic counting machinery (Witt / Lyndon basis) applies unchanged to k pieces: with k generators the number of conditions at order n is M(k,n) = (1/n) Σ_{d|n} μ(d) k^{n/d}, unless relations hold among the pieces. For three pairwise pieces of a 3-body problem the generic (relation-free) per-grade counts are 3, 3, 8, 18, 48, 116, …
- **Comparison with this repository's "level" sequence [3, 6, 17, 116] for the pairwise 3-body Poisson algebra (my inference, assuming "level L" = span of all brackets of nesting depth ≤ L):** in the free Lie algebra on 3 generators the depth-≤2 span is generators (3) + [g,g] (3) + [g,[g,g]] (8) + [[g,g],[g,g]] (3, these three grade-4 elements are linearly independent in L_4) = 17, which I verified computationally. So the pairwise algebra being 17-dimensional at level 2 is exactly what a *free* Lie algebra on the three pairwise Hamiltonians would give; the first possible deviation from freeness would show at level 3 (grades 4–8). This is my own observation, not a published statement, and depends on the repository's precise definition of "level".
- In the N-body pairwise splitting H = Σ_{i<j} K_ij with K_ij containing (parts of) T_i, T_j and V_ij, pieces sharing a body do not commute and each piece is itself non-separable in the RKN sense relative to the others, so the RKN relation does not transfer directly; Dehnen–Hernandez's finding that three-body terms enter at second order is consistent with [K_ij, K_jk] ≠ 0 being the leading error. No source makes a Lie-algebraic dimension statement about this.

### Gaps
- I found no paper (Wisdom–Holman, Chambers/Mercury, Duncan–Levison–Lee/SyMBA, Hernandez–Bertschinger, Dehnen–Hernandez, REBOUND/WHFast) that counts independent order conditions or computes the dimension of the Lie algebra generated by pairwise or Kepler sub-Hamiltonians. Full texts were inaccessible, so a count buried in an appendix cannot be excluded, but no search snippet or abstract hints at one.
- Blanes et al. 2013 (arXiv 1208.0689) does count conditions for the near-integrable two-piece case (Jacobi vs Poincaré) but its tables could not be read; this is the closest published analogue of "a relation among the pieces changes the count by one".

## Key question 4: Is there a published statement that the Lie algebra generated by pairwise sub-Hamiltonians T_i + T_j + V_ij is "free modulo one relation at length 4" (or equivalent)?

### Takeaway
No. Across all searches no published statement of this kind was found, neither in the numerical-analysis splitting literature nor in the celestial-mechanics integrator literature. The only well-documented "one relation at length 4" statement is the RKN relation [B,[B,[B,A]]] = 0 for a *two*-piece kinetic/potential splitting.

### Cited Findings
- The only explicit relation statements located are the RKN ones: "[F_b,[F_b,[F_a,F_b]]] ≡ 0" ([ScienceDirect, Blanes–Casas–Escorihuela-Tomàs 2022](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0168927422001842)) and "F_{1112} = 0 in (2.21)" ([arXiv 2401.01722](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.01722)), both for H = T(p) + V(q).
- Searches for "Lie algebra generated by two-body Hamiltonians", "pairwise Kepler splitting free Lie algebra", "nested Poisson brackets vanish three-body terms" returned only unrelated material (Lie–Poisson structure of the reduced n-body problem, sp(2n−2) — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/230568236_The_Lie-Poisson_structure_of_the_reduced_n-body_problem); Keplerian-based splitting — [OUP MNRAS](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/440/1/719/1747624)).
- The McLachlan–Quispel 2002 survey ([Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/acta-numerica/article/splitting-methods/122F5736DAF3D88598989E68FE4D2EF2)) and McLachlan's "The Lie algebra of classical mechanics" ([arXiv 1905.07554](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.07554)) surfaced as the places where the structure of the Lie algebra generated by kinetic and potential energy is studied; the latter (McLachlan & Ryland?/McLachlan 2019) studies exactly the Lie algebra generated by T and V, and is the natural citation for "the Lie algebra of classical mechanics is the free Lie algebra on two generators modulo the ideal generated by [B,[B,[B,A]]] plus possibly further relations" — but its content could not be read here and the claim is from memory of its abstract, so treat as unverified.

### Inferences
- For a kinetic/potential two-piece split the relevant object is L(A,B)/⟨[B,[B,[B,A]]]⟩ with per-degree dimensions 2, 1, 2, 2, 4, 5, 10, 15, 26, 42 (my computation, KQ2). Any analogous statement for pairwise sub-Hamiltonians would have to be checked directly, e.g. by computing the rank of all grade-4 brackets of K_12, K_13, K_23 (generic 18 for a free algebra on 3 generators) in the same way this repository computes level dimensions; the level-2 count 17 being exactly the free count suggests no relation of grade ≤ 4 among the pairwise pieces except possibly inside [L_2, L_3]-type grade-5 or deeper terms that level 2 does not probe. (Inference, not a published result.)

### Gaps
- McLachlan, "The Lie algebra of classical mechanics" (arXiv 1905.07554, J. Comput. Dyn. 2019) is very likely the key reference for the exact structure (additional relations beyond [B,[B,[B,A]]] = 0 in the algebra generated by T and V, and its dimension sequence). It could not be fetched; the report writer should flag it for direct consultation.
- No source answers the T_i + T_j + V_ij "free modulo one relation" question; this appears to be an open / unpublished point.
