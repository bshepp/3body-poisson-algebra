## Key Question 1 — Which published integrators split the N-body problem into pairwise or per-pair Kepler sub-problems?

### Takeaway
Three families genuinely split the N-body Hamiltonian into per-pair pieces: (i) SAKURA (Gonçalves Ferrari, Boekholt & Portegies Zwart 2014) — H = Σ_i T_i + ½Σ_{i≠j} (K_ij − T_ij), with K_ij the two-body Kepler Hamiltonian of the pair; (ii) Hernandez & Bertschinger 2015 (HB15) and its analysis/extension by Dehnen & Hernandez 2017 and Hernandez 2016 — H = T + Σ_{i<j}(K_ij − T_ij), a *sequential* (non-parallel) product of per-pair Kepler flows and per-pair "negative drifts"; (iii) SyMBA (Duncan, Levison & Lee 1998) and MERCURY (Chambers 1999), which split the *potential* per pair into radial shells / switching-function pieces but keep a single Kepler (Sun–planet) part. Wisdom–Holman 1991, WHFast, Laskar & Robutel 2001, Blanes et al. 2013, Farrés et al. 2013 and Rein, Tamayo & Brown 2019 all use the two-piece A + εB split only.

### Cited Findings

**Hernandez & Bertschinger 2015 (MNRAS 452, 1934; arXiv:1503.02728) — pairwise Kepler split, sequential.**
- Eq. (3): "H = T + V = T + ΣΣ_{i, j>i} V_ij = T + ΣΣ_{i, j>i} (K_ij − T_ij). Here T is the kinetic energy, V is the potential energy from Newton's gravitational law, V_ij is the pairwise potential of particles, T_ij is the kinetic energy of particle i plus particle j, and K_ij is the two body Hamiltonian for particles i and j (K stands for Kepler) including both relative and center of mass terms." — [arXiv:1503.02728](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.02728), Sec. 2.2.
- Eq. (4): "φ_h = Π_{(i,j) pairs} (exp hD_{K_ij} exp −hD_{T_ij}) exp(hD_T) = Π_{(i,j) pairs} (ψ^{3ij}_h ψ^{2ij}_{−h}) ψ^1_h ... Here D_A is defined by D_A f = {f, A}". The map is a product over *all* N(N−1)/2 pairs of a Kepler flow and a backward pair-drift, applied sequentially (not in parallel): "Like SAKURA we decompose the N-body problem into two-body problems. In contrast to SAKURA, our two-body problems are not independent." — [arXiv:1503.02728](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.02728), Sec. 1 & 2.2.
- Number of pieces: the symmetric version φ²_h = φ†_{h/2} φ_{h/2} uses "N(N−1) total Kepler solvers, where N is the number of particles" (two per pair). — [arXiv:1503.02728](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.02728), Sec. 1.
- Appendix A on SAKURA: "The reason why SAKURA is not time reversible is that the Kepler steps in (A1) are not done in serial but instead are done in parallel, using the initial phase space coordinates for every Kepler evaluation." — [arXiv:1503.02728](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.02728), Appendix A.

**Gonçalves Ferrari, Boekholt & Portegies Zwart 2014 (MNRAS 440, 719; arXiv:1402.3325) — SAKURA, pairwise Kepler split, parallel.**
- Eqs. (2)–(8): "H_T ≡ Σ H_{T_i}, H_{T_i} ≡ ½ m_i v_i²" ; "H_U ≡ ½ΣΣ_{i≠j} H_{U_ij}, H_{U_ij} ≡ −m_i m_j / r_ij" ; "H_{K_ij} ≡ μ_ij (½ v_ij² − (m_i+m_j)/r_ij) ... is the 2-body Keplerian Hamiltonian and H_{T_ij} ≡ ½ μ_ij v_ij²" ; "H = H_T + H_W = Σ_i H_{T_i} + ½ΣΣ_{i≠j} H_{W_ij}, with H_{W_ij} = H_{K_ij} − H_{T_ij} ≡ H_{U_ij}." — [arXiv:1402.3325](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.3325), Sec. 2.1.
- Motivation: "a way to split the Hamiltonian of an N-body system, which is based on two main arguments: i) the validity of the superposition principle, and ii) the existence of an analytical solution for the 2-body problem." — [arXiv:1402.3325](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.3325), Sec. 2.1.

**Dehnen & Hernandez 2017 (MNRAS 465, 1201; arXiv:1609.09375) — analysis and 4th-order extension of pairwise Kepler maps.**
- Abstract: "We study analytically and experimentally certain symplectic and time-reversible N-body integrators which employ a Kepler solver for each pair-wise interaction, including the method of Hernandez & Bertschinger. Owing to the Kepler solver, these methods treat close two-body interactions correctly, while close three-body encounters contribute to the truncation error at second order and above. The second-order errors can be corrected to obtain a fourth-order scheme with little computational overhead. We generalise this map to an integrator which employs a Kepler solver only for selected interactions" — [arXiv:1609.09375](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.09375).
- The paper indexes the per-pair pieces as V_n ≡ V_{i_n j_n}, n = 1..K (K = number of pairs), and derives the error Hamiltonian by applying BCH pair by pair (Appendix A1, eqs. A8–A13). — [arXiv:1609.09375](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.09375), Appendix A1.

**Hernandez 2016 (MNRAS 458, 4285; arXiv:1511.07476) — HB15 with a chosen subset of pairs in the Kepler group.**
- "The pairs of particles are divided into a kick or Kepler [group] ... the most efficient choice is that the four planet-sun pairs go in the Kepler group, while the other 6 pairs go to the kick [group]" (5-body test). Also notes for WH-type maps "The commutator [B_h, C_h] = 0 for WHD and WHJ ... but generally [B_h, C_h] ≠ 0 for HB15." — [arXiv:1511.07476](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.07476), Sec. 2.

**SyMBA (Duncan, Levison & Lee 1998, AJ 116, 2067) — per-pair potential split into radial shells (secondary description).**
- Hernandez & Dehnen 2024 describe DLL98's split: "we decompose the potential into V = Σ_{i=0}^∞ V_i = Ṽ_0 + (Ṽ_1 − Ṽ_0) + (Ṽ_2 − Ṽ_1) + ..., where V_0 = Ṽ_0, V_1 = Ṽ_1 − Ṽ_0, ..." with cutoff radii r_1 > r_2 > ... of constant ratio R, each shell having its own time step h_i with h_i/h_{i+1} = M; the method "forms the basis of the SYMBA code (DLL98)". They also quote DLL98's own error Hamiltonian, "also derived in eq. (35) of DLL98: H_err^{DLL98} = (h_0²/12){{H_Sun, H_Kep}, H_Kep + ½H_Sun} + Σ_i (h_i²/12){{V_i, H_Kep}, H_Kep + ½ V_i + Σ_{j>i} V_j} + O(h⁴)", and the dominant per-pair term during an encounter "H_err^{(i,j)} = (h_k²/24){{V_{k ij}, H_Kep}, V_{k ij}} + O(h_k⁴)". — [arXiv:2401.07113](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07113), Sec. 2 and Sec. 4.3.2 (eqs. 35–36). ADS record of DLL98 itself not reachable in this session.

**MERCURY (Chambers 1999, MNRAS 304, 793) — per-pair switching function (secondary description).**
- Web-search summary of the method: "A function K(r_ij) moves the large pairwise terms from the perturbing part to the Keplerian part. K(r_ij) tends to zero when r_ij is small, while tending to one when r_ij is large" — [Chambers 1999 at OUP](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/304/4/793/1047461) (abstract only; full text not fetched). Rein et al. 2019 ("MERCURIUS", arXiv:1903.04972) discuss inconsistencies in the original split (not fetched).

**Two-piece (A + εB) methods only — explicit negative statements:**
- Wisdom–Holman family as described in Rein, Tamayo & Brown 2019: "keep track of it by formally replacing B in the Hamiltonian with εB such that the splitting is of the form H = A + εB"; "WH = e^{½A} e^{B} e^{½A}" (eq. 15). — [arXiv:1907.11335](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.11335), Sec. 2.2.
- Laskar & Robutel 2001: "as H = A + εB, the Campbell-Baker-Hausdorf (CBH) theorem ensures that e^{τL_H} = e^{τL_A} e^{τL_{εB}} + o(τ)"; all SABA_n/SBAB_n schemes are compositions of e^{cτL_A} and e^{dτL_{εB}} only. — [arXiv:astro-ph/0005074](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0005074), Sec. 2–3.
- Blanes, Casas, Farrés, Laskar, Makazaga & Murua 2013: "H(q,p;ε) = H^{[a]}(q,p) + εH^{[b]}(q,p), where ε ≪ 1 and H^{[a]} is exactly integrable" with H^{[a]} = H_K the sum of Keplerian motions "of each planet around the central mass" in Jacobi or heliocentric coordinates. — [arXiv:1208.0689](https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.0689), Sec. 1, eq. (2).
- Farrés et al. 2013: same two-piece split, "S_r(τ) = Π exp(a_i τA) exp(εb_i τB)". — [arXiv:1208.0716](https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.0716), eq. (14).
- WHFast (Rein & Tamayo 2015, arXiv:1506.01084): two-piece WH split; the full text contains no BCH/commutator discussion at all (grep for "commutator", "Poisson bracket", "BCH", "Baker" returns nothing; "error term" refers only to numerical round-off classes E_bound/E_rand). — [arXiv:1506.01084](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.01084).

### Inferences
- Only the HB15/Dehnen–Hernandez line and SAKURA split the *Hamiltonian itself* into exactly-solvable two-body Kepler Hamiltonians per pair. SyMBA/MERCURY split only the *potential* per pair (kinetic energy stays global), so their pieces are V_ij-type (each a function of positions only; all mutually commuting), not Kepler problems.
- For HB15 the pieces generating the Lie algebra are {T, K_ij (or equivalently V_ij), T_ij}; because the product in eq. (4) is sequential, pair orderings matter in principle, which is exactly what HB15 investigate ("we found no generally preferred ordering in the product of eq. (4)").

## Key Question 2 — Is there a table/statement of the number of independent error terms (commutators) per order, and is it discussed that commutators of disjoint pairs vanish?

### Takeaway
No N-body paper found gives a *count* of independent commutators per order for a pairwise splitting. Hernandez & Dehnen 2017 (planetary four-piece split) do list "all possible O(h²) terms" (8 of them) and state "all other independent nested Poisson brackets of these basic building blocks vanish." Dehnen & Hernandez 2017 explicitly state that pair–pair brackets {{T,V_ij},V_kl} vanish when all four indices differ, that nested brackets of two pieces at order h⁴ "{TV⁴} and {TV³T} vanish", and classify the surviving terms by the number of distinct particles (2-, 3-, 4-body). Per-order counts for general two-piece splittings (2,1,2,3,6,9,18,30,56,99,186) and for the RKN case [A,[A,[A,B]]]=0 (2,1,2,2,4,5,10,14,25,39,69) appear in the Blanes–Casas–Murua 2024 review, and the near-integrable counts "1 term of order εh^k, ½(k−1) of order ε²h^k, ⅙(k−1)(k−2) of order ε³h^k" are attributed to McLachlan 1995.

### Cited Findings

**Dehnen & Hernandez 2017 (arXiv:1609.09375) — vanishing of disjoint-pair brackets, classification by particle count, at orders h² and h⁴.**
- Sec. 2.2, after eq. (13): "The second contribution to the errors in equations (12) are sums over terms of the form {{T, V_ij}, V_lk}. These vanish if all four indices differ, and the only non-vanishing terms are of two types: {{T,V_ij},V_ij} = G²m_i m_j(m_i+m_j)/r_ij⁴ (14a) and {{T,V_ij},V_ik} = G²m_i m_j m_k (x_ij·x_ik)/(r_ij³ r_ik³) with j ≠ k (14b). ... The second type of terms (equation 14b) becomes large only in a close three-body encounter between particles i, j, and k (close encounters of more than three particles only contribute to yet higher-order error terms, see Appendix B1)." They define {{T,V},V}₂ ≡ Σ_{i<j}{{T,V_ij},V_ij} and {{T,V},V}₃ ≡ 2Σ_{i<j<k}[{{T,V_ij},V_ik} + {{T,V_jk},V_ji} + {{T,V_ki},V_kj}] with {{T,V},V} = {{T,V},V}₂ + {{T,V},V}₃ (eqs. 15a,b). — [arXiv:1609.09375](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.09375).
- Sec. 3 (map [DB]²): "H_err = (h²/48){{T,V},V}₃ + O(h⁴)" (eq. 28), and: "all O(h²) error terms arising from close two-body encounters have been removed. The only remaining terms are of the form (14b), which account for close three-body encounters. In fact, this is true at all orders: error terms which are (nested) Poisson brackets containing only T and V_ij are eliminated at all orders. This can be seen by letting m_k → 0 for all but one pair of particles, when the method becomes exact for this pair while the Hamiltonian collapses to (22). Thus, the only remaining error terms involve two or more particle pairs." — [arXiv:1609.09375](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.09375).
- Appendix A1, eqs. (A12)–(A13): the pairwise-sequential error Hamiltonian is written as a sum over ordered pairs of pairs, "H_err^{[BD]²} = (h²/24) Σ_{n=1}^{K} Σ_{k=1}^{n−1} {{T, V_{i_n j_n}}, V_{i_k j_k}} + O(h⁴). The double sum in this last form includes each pair of pairs {{T,V_ij},V_lk} exactly once, except for those with (i,j) = (l,k) which are not contained at all. The term {{T,V},V}, which occurs in the error Hamiltonian of the leapfrog, contains each pair of pairs {{T,V_ij},V_lk} twice, except for (i,j) = (l,k) which are contained once. Thus, the form (A12) is identical to (h²/48)[{{T,V},V} − {{T,V},V}₂] + O(h⁴) = (h²/48){{T,V},V}₃ + O(h⁴)." — [arXiv:1609.09375](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.09375).
- Appendix B1 "The fourth-order error terms": "Note that the nested Poisson brackets {TV⁴} and {TV³T} vanish (regardless of their coefficients). The r[emaining terms can] be split into contributions from two-, three-, and four-body encounters: {VT³V} = {VT³V}₂ + {VT³V}₃, {T[VTVT] = ...}₂ + ...₃, and {VTVTV} = {VTVTV}₂ + {VTVTV}₃ + {VTVTV}₄" with explicit sums, e.g. "{VTVTV}₄ = ΣΣΣ_{n, k≠n, l≠n,k} {V_n T V_k T V_l}", and the elementary 2-body building blocks {V_ij T⁴}, {V_ij T³ V_ij}, {T V_ij T V_ij T}, {V_ij T V_ij T V_ij} given in closed form. (Here {X₁X₂…X_n} is their shorthand for the left-nested bracket.) — [arXiv:1609.09375](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.09375), Appendix B1.
- Appendix C2 (commutation of disjoint pair maps, as a *parallelisation* remark, not an error-count remark): "two individual maps ψ_{W_ij} and ψ_{W_kl} are mutually independent and can be swapped or even computed simultaneously if all four indices differ." — [arXiv:1609.09375](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.09375).
- Sec. 2.3 mentions the standard vanishing identity indirectly: a sixth-order forward integrator needs backward steps "unless the error term {{{{V,T},T},T},V} can be integrated (Chin 2005)" — i.e. the fact that {{{V,T},T},T} ≠ 0 but {{{T,V},V},V}... (the identity [V,[V,[V,T]]] = 0 itself is *not* written out). — [arXiv:1609.09375](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.09375).

Sec. 10: "when A is quadratic in the actions p, and B depends only on the positions q ... {{A,B},B} depends only on q and is thus integrable" (basis of the SABAC_n correctors). The identity {{{A,B},B},B} = 0 is not written out explicitly in the paper. — [arXiv:astro-ph/0005074](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0005074).
- Farrés et al. 2013, eq. (15) repeats the same expansion with coefficients p_{i,j}; Sec. 5.2.1: "Since in Jacobi coordinates A is quadratic in p and B depends only of q, then it follows that the term [[A,B],B] depends only on q". — [arXiv:1208.0716](https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.0716).
- Rein, Tamayo & Brown 2019, eq. (16): "H' = A + εB − (dt²/24){A,{A,εB}} − ε²(dt²/12){B,{A,B}} + O(dt⁴) + O(ε²dt⁴) ... There are two terms, one is O(εdt²), the other is O(ε²dt²)"; Sec. 2.3.2: "{B,{B,A}} evaluates to a combination of spatial derivatives of the accelerations ... B̂ = B + dt²(1/24){B,{B,A}}" (modified kick). No per-order counts; two pieces only. — [arXiv:1907.11335](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.11335).

### Inferences
- The closest thing in the N-body literature to "counting independent commutators of per-pair pieces" is Dehnen & Hernandez 2017's bookkeeping by *number of distinct particles* (subscripts ₂, ₃, ₄ on the error terms) together with the explicit statement that brackets of disjoint pairs vanish and that {TV⁴}, {TV³T} vanish identically. This is a classification of *non-vanishing term types*, not a dimension count of the generated Lie algebra.
- Hernandez & Dehnen 2017's "8 non-vanishing O(h²) terms, all others vanish" for the four-generator planetary split is the only explicit exhaustive enumeration found of independent nested brackets at a given order for a multi-piece (>2) N-body splitting; it is derived by hand, not from a free-Lie-algebra argument.
- All papers found use the same "number of distinct particles" logic as Childs et al.'s locality (KQ4), but none connects it to graded dimensions.

### Gaps
- No paper found states the number of independent error commutators per order for the HB15/SAKURA pairwise Kepler split (searched the full texts of arXiv:1503.02728, 1402.3325, 1609.09375, 1511.07476, 2401.07113 for "number of", "independent", "free Lie", "dimension"; only the "all other independent nested Poisson brackets ... vanish" sentence in arXiv:1612.05329 and the "vanish if all four indices differ" sentence in arXiv:1609.09375 came up).
- SAKURA's paper contains no BCH / Poisson-bracket error analysis at all (full-text grep for "commutator", "Baker", "BCH", "Poisson bracket", "error Hamiltonian" returned nothing); it is purely a construction + numerical tests paper.

## Key Question 3 — Does any paper describe the Lie algebra generated by the pieces as free modulo specific relations and compute its graded dimensions?

### Takeaway
No. For two pieces, the celestial-mechanics papers invoke the free Lie algebra L(A,B) with a Hall or Lyndon basis (Laskar & Robutel 2001; Blanes et al. 2013; Farrés et al. 2013) and the 2024 review tabulates the dimensions of the free algebra and of the quotient by [A,[A,[A,B]]]=0; for pairwise/per-interaction N-body splits no paper found describes the Lie algebra "free modulo pair-disjointness relations" or computes its graded dimensions.

### Cited Findings
- Blanes et al. 2013: "the free Lie algebra L(C₁,C₂,C₃,...) generated by the non-commuting indeterminates C₁,C₂,C₃,..., admits a basis (the Lyndon basis [19]) in one-to-one correspondence with the set of Lyndon multi-indices ... Lazard elimination theorem [19] shows that, as vector spaces, the direct sum of L(C₁,C₂,C₃,...) with the linear span of A is isomorphic to L(A,B) (sending C₁,C₂,C₃,... to B, [A,B], ½[A,[A,B]], ... respectively)." — [arXiv:1208.0689](https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.0689), Sec. 2.4.
- Blanes, Casas & Murua 2024: the RKN case is treated as the graded Lie algebra with the relation [F₁,[F₁,[F₁,F₂]]] = 0 ("C_k = 0 for k ≥ 4") plus further relations "([C₃,[C₂,C₃]] vanishes identically)", with the resulting dimensions d_n tabulated (Table 2.1) and the note that McLachlan & Murua 2019 "conjectured (and checked up to order 20)" that two related classes give the same reduction. — [arXiv:2401.01722](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.01722), Sec. 3.1.
- Laskar & Robutel 2001: "We will call L(U,V) the free Lie algebra generated by U and V, endowed with its canonical associative structure. We will also use the symbol ≡ for the equality in L(U,V) modulo terms of degree ≥ 2 in V" — i.e. they work in the quotient of the free Lie algebra by the ideal of terms of degree ≥ 2 in the perturbation, to derive the SABA_n/SBAB_n coefficients at any order (Proposition 1 & Gauss–Lobatto nodes). — [arXiv:astro-ph/0005074](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0005074), Sec. 5 and 7.
- Dehnen & Hernandez 2017 and Hernandez & Dehnen 2017: classify brackets by particle content and list non-vanishing ones (see KQ2) but never phrase this as a Lie algebra with relations, nor give dimensions. — [arXiv:1609.09375](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.09375); [arXiv:1612.05329](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.05329).
- Web search for "free Lie algebra" + N-body + pairwise/Kepler/disjoint returned only the HB15/Dehnen–Hernandez/REBOUND papers already covered; the search tool itself reported "The search didn't turn up a source that mentions a 'free Lie algebra' or the specific claim that commutators vanish for disjoint pairs" in that context. — [search result list](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.09375) (the HB15/DH17/RTB19 hits).

### Inferences
- The N-body community's Lie-algebraic machinery is entirely two-generator (L(A,B), with ε-grading or the RKN relation). The step to a many-generator algebra with "locality" relations (disjoint pairs commute) has not been taken in the celestial-mechanics literature, as far as the sources examined show.

### Gaps
- McLachlan 1995 ("Composition methods in the presence of small parameters", BIT) and McLachlan & Quispel 2002 (Acta Numerica), McLachlan & Murua 2019 — the primary sources for the c_n, d_n counts — were not fetched; the counts are quoted via the 2024 review.
- Not checked: Koseleff's 1993 thesis / papers on Lyndon bases in symplectic integration; Chin's forward-integrator papers (the "{{{{V,T},T},T},V} integrable" remark).

## Key Question 4 — Quantum many-body: Childs, Su, Tran, Wiebe & Zhu, "Theory of Trotter error with commutator scaling" (PRX 11, 011020, 2021; arXiv:1912.08854)

### Takeaway
Yes to counting nested commutators of the summands and yes to using locality (operators with non-overlapping supports commute) to reduce the count; no to any free-Lie-algebra / Witt-formula dimension counting — their quantity α̃_comm is a *sum of norms over all (p+1)-tuples of summand indices*, not a count of linearly independent commutators.

### Cited Findings
- Theorem 11 (Sec. 3.4): "Let H = Σ_{γ=1}^Γ H_γ be an operator consisting of Γ summands and t ≥ 0. Let S(t) = Π_{υ=1}^Υ Π_{γ=1}^Γ e^{t a(υ,γ) H_{π_υ(γ)}} be a pth-order product formula. Define α̃_comm = Σ_{γ₁,γ₂,...,γ_{p+1}=1}^Γ ‖[H_{γ_{p+1}}, ··· [H_{γ₂}, H_{γ₁}]]‖. Then, the additive Trotter error and the multiplicative Trotter error ... can be asymptotically bounded as ‖A(t)‖ = O(α̃_comm t^{p+1} e^{2tΥΣ‖H_γ‖}), ‖M(t)‖ = O(α̃_comm t^{p+1} e^{2tΥΣ‖H_γ‖}). Furthermore, if the H_γ are anti-Hermitian ... ‖A(t)‖ = O(α̃_comm t^{p+1}), ‖M(t)‖ = O(α̃_comm t^{p+1})." — [arXiv:1912.08854](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.08854), Sec. 3.4 (eqs. 54–55).
- Sec. 1.3: "We emphasize that this theorem does not follow from truncating the BCH series. Although the (p+1)st-order term of the BCH series is also a linear combination of nested commutators similar to α̃_comm, such a term can be dominated by a higher-order term when t is fixed, as is the case for nearest-neighbor lattice systems ... Truncating the BCH series ignores significant, potentially dominant error contributions". — [arXiv:1912.08854](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.08854).
- Locality used to reduce the count — k-local Hamiltonians, Sec. 4.1: "each H_{j₁,...,j_k} acts nontrivially only on qubits j₁,...,j_k ... To compare with the product-formula algorithm, we need to analyze the nested commutators [H_{γ_{p+1}}, ··· [H_{γ₂}, H_{γ₁}]], where each H_γ is some local operator H_{j₁,...,j_k}. In order for this commutator to be nonzero, every operator must have support that overlaps with the support of operators from the inner layers. Using this idea, we estimate that α̃_comm = Σ ‖[H_{γ_{p+1}}, ··· [H_{γ₂}, H_{γ₁}]]‖ = O(|||H|||₁^p ‖H‖₁), where |||H|||₁ = max_l max_{j_l} Σ_{j₁,...,j_{l−1},j_{l+1},...,j_k} ‖H_{j₁,...,j_k}‖ is the induced 1-norm." (eq. 64) — [arXiv:1912.08854](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.08854), Sec. 4.1.
- Electronic structure (plane-wave dual basis, summands T, U, V): "We compute the norm of [H_{γ_{p+1}}, ··· [H_{γ₂}, H_{γ₁}]], H_γ ∈ {T, U, V} by induction. We show in Appendix D that α̃_comm = Σ ‖[...]‖ = O(n^{p+1})." (eq. 60) — [arXiv:1912.08854](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.08854), Sec. 4.1.
- Power-law / nearest-neighbour: truncated H̃ "is a 2-local Hamiltonian with 1-norm ‖H̃‖₁ = O(n) and induced 1-norm |||H̃|||₁ = O(1). Theorem 11 and Corollary 12 then imply that a Trotter number of r = O(n^{1/p} t^{1+1/p}/ε^{1/p}) suffices" — i.e. α̃_comm = O(n) for geometrically local lattice Hamiltonians. — [arXiv:1912.08854](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.08854), Sec. 4.1.
- Clustered Hamiltonians use an explicit commutation structure: "[H_{2k}, H_{2k'}] = 0 and [H_{2k+1}, H_{2k'+1}] = 0 for all k, k'. Using Theorem 11, we show in Appendix H ..." — [arXiv:1912.08854](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.08854), Sec. 4.2.
- Tight prefactor computations for the Heisenberg chain (Sec. 5): "For the even-odd ordering, we need to compute all the nested commutators of A and B. We do this by fixing one term X_{2j−1}X_{2j} + Y_{2j−1}Y_{2j} + Z_{2j−1}Z_{2j} + h_{2j−1}Z_{2j−1} of A in the innermost layer and simplifying all the outer terms using geometrical locality. We then apply the triangle inequality to analyze the summation of terms over j = 1,...,⌊n/2⌋." — [arXiv:1912.08854](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.08854), Sec. 5.
- Contrast with earlier locality-based approach: "For Hamiltonians with nearest-neighbor interactions, Ref. [25] gave an argument based on locality to cancel the majority of the Trotter error. However, this approach reduces to the worst-case scenario for systems lacking geometric locality." — [arXiv:1912.08854](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.08854), Sec. 1.2.
- **Negative result (explicit search):** the full text of arXiv:1912.08854 contains no occurrence of "free Lie", "Witt", "Lyndon", "Hall basis" or "linearly independent" (grep over the 2800-line pdftotext output). The authors state their bound "only contains a finite number of error terms" but never count *independent* commutators; the sum in α̃_comm ranges over all Γ^{p+1} index tuples (with repetitions and no antisymmetry/Jacobi reduction), and reductions come from vanishing of norms of commutators of non-overlapping terms, not from Lie-algebraic independence. — [arXiv:1912.08854](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.08854).

### Inferences
- Childs et al.'s "support must overlap with inner layers" is precisely the mechanism that in the gravitational setting makes {{T,V_ij},V_kl} = 0 for disjoint pairs (Dehnen & Hernandez 2017) — but in the N-body case the kinetic energy T is a global (all-particle) summand, so overlap is automatic whenever T is in the innermost position, which is why the pairwise Kepler split (K_ij containing only the pair's kinetic energy) is what makes disjoint-pair commutators genuinely vanish.
- Neither community reports graded dimensions of the "local" Lie algebra; both count/bound *terms* (Childs: by norm sums over index tuples; Dehnen–Hernandez: by particle content).

### Gaps
- Did not check the follow-up quantum literature (e.g. Layden 2022 "First-order Trotter error from a second-order perspective", Chen & Brandão / Tran et al. on locality, or Suzuki-type "Lie algebra of local terms" works) for explicit free-Lie-algebra counts — outside the ~15-call budget.
- Appendix D of Childs et al. (the induction giving O(n^{p+1})) was not read in detail.
