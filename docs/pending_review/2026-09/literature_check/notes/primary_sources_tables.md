## Key Question 1 — McLachlan & Murua 2019 (arXiv:1905.07554): exact definition, structure theorem, tables, entropy, erratum, conjecture, multi-generator mentions

### Takeaway
The "Lie algebra of classical mechanics" L_P(A,B) is NOT simply Free(A,B)/<[B,[B,[B,A]]]>; it is the free Lie algebra on two generators A (degree 2) and B (degree 0) in the class P of "polynomially graded" Lie algebras, i.e. L(A,B)/I where I is the Lie ideal generated by ALL brackets of degree-0 elements [L^0(A,B), L^0(A,B)]. Its structure is span(X) ⊕ L(A,[X,A]) with X ≅ free commutative non-associative algebra on one generator (rooted full binary trees); dimensions by order n = 1..20 are 2,1,2,2,4,5,10,14,25,39,69,110,193,320,555,938,1630,2786,4852,8370; entropy 1.8249111600523655937…; the 2003 construction is stated to be in error from order 13. The paper treats exactly two generators; the only N-body content is the mention of near-integrable solar-system methods in the introduction.

### Cited Findings

**Bibliographic.** Title "The Lie algebra of classical mechanics", Robert I. McLachlan and Ander Murua, arXiv:1905.07554v1 [math-ph] 18 May 2019 — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.07554); full text [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.07554), [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1905.07554).

**Abstract (verbatim excerpt):** "We show that it is the direct sum of an abelian algebra X, spanned by 'modified' potential energies isomorphic to the free commutative nonassociative algebra with one generator, and an algebra freely generated by the kinetic energy and its Poisson bracket with X. We calculate the dimensions c_n of its homogeneous subspaces and determine the value of its entropy lim_{n→∞} c_n^{1/n}. It is 1.8249…, a fundamental constant associated to classical mechanics. We conjecture that the class of systems with Euclidean kinetic energy metrics is already free, i.e., the only linear identities satisfied by the Lie brackets of all such systems are those satisfied by the Lie algebra of classical mechanics." — [arXiv:1905.07554](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.07554)

**Statement that the 2003 paper is in error (two places, verbatim):**
- §1: "This 'Lie algebra of classical mechanics' was introduced in [13]. However, that paper has an error that is corrected here." ([13] = McLachlan & Ryland, J. Math. Phys. 44 (2003), 3071–3087.) — [arXiv:1905.07554 §1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.07554)
- §1: "Bases for the homogeneous subspaces of this Lie algebra have been constructed for small orders [3, 4, 15], and McLachlan and Ryland [13] attempted to construct the entire Lie algebra. However, their construction is in error starting at order 13." — [arXiv:1905.07554 §1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.07554)

**The simplest identity (Eq. 2) and its derivation, verbatim:** "There are always identities satisfied by the Poisson brackets of the kinetic and potential energy. The simplest of these is {V,{V,{V,T}}} ≡ 0. (2) For, working in local coordinates (p,q) with T = ½ pᵀM(q)p … {V,T} = Σ_{i,j} ∂V/∂q_i M_ij(q) p_j is of degree 1 in p, and … {V,{V,T}} = Σ_{i,j} ∂V/∂q_i M_ij ∂V/∂q_j (3) is a function of q only. So V and {V,{V,T}} commute." — [arXiv:1905.07554 §1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.07554)

**Notation (§2.1):** "[XY] := [X,Y], [XYZ] := [X,[Y,Z]], [XⁿY] = [X,[X^{n−1},Y]], and for sets X,Y, [XY] := [X,Y] := {[X,Y] : X ∈ X, Y ∈ Y}." — [arXiv:1905.07554 §2.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.07554)

**Definition 1 (class P, verbatim):** "A Lie algebra L is of class P ('polynomially graded') if it is graded, i.e. L = ⊕_{n≥0} L_n, and its homogeneous subspaces L_n satisfy [L_n, L_m] ⊆ L_{n+m−1} if n > 0 or m > 0; and [L_0, L_0] = 0 (4). Note that this implies [(L_0)^{n+1} L_n] = 0 (5) for all n. We call the grading of L its grading by degree." — [arXiv:1905.07554 §2.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.07554)

**Definition 2 (free in class P; the object itself, verbatim):** "Let F be a Lie algebra of class P generated by a set X. Then F is called a free Lie algebra in the class P over the set X, if for any Lie algebra R of class P, every mapping X → R that respects the degrees of the elements can be extended to a unique homomorphism F → R of Lie algebras of class P. We denote as L_P(A,B) the free Lie algebra in the class P where A has degree 2 and B has degree 0, and we call it the Lie algebra of classical mechanics." — [arXiv:1905.07554 §2.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.07554)

**Definition 3 (degree grading on the free Lie algebra) and the quotient description, verbatim:** "L(A,B) = ⊕_{n≥0} L^n(A,B) such that A ∈ L^2(A,B), B ∈ L^0(A,B), and [L^n(A,B), L^m(A,B)] ⊆ L^{min(n+m−1,0)}(A,B), for all n,m ≥ 0. [sic — printed as min; see note in Inferences] … Note that we have L_P(A,B) = L(A,B)/I, where I is the Lie ideal generated by [L^0(A,B), L^0(A,B)], and that L_P(A,B) inherits a grading by degree … In addition to the grading by degree, L_P(A,B) also carries the standard grading which we call the grading by order, generated by order(A) = order(B) = 1 and order([Y,Z]) = order(Y) + order(Z)." — [arXiv:1905.07554 §2.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.07554)

**Definition 4 (realisation map):** "Given smooth functions T(p,q) = ½ M(q)(p,p) and V = V(q) (p,q ∈ R^d), Φ_{T,V} : L_P(A,B) → C^∞_pol(R^{2d}) is the unique homomorphism of Lie algebras of class P such that Φ_{T,V}(A) = T and Φ_{T,V}(B) = V." (C^∞_pol = smooth functions polynomial in p, under the Poisson bracket, "of class P, with T of degree 2 and V of degree 0".) — [arXiv:1905.07554 §2.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.07554)

**Theorem 1 (structure theorem, verbatim):** "The Lie algebra L_P(A,B) of classical mechanics satisfies L_P(A,B) ≅ span(X) ⊕ L(A,[X,A]), (6) where span(X) is abelian, of degree 0, and is isomorphic as a vector space to the free commutative nonassociative algebra generated by B under the operation U₁ ∗ U₂ := [U₂,[U₁,A]]. (7) Specifically, X = {B, B∗B, (B∗B)∗B, (B∗B)∗(B∗B), ((B∗B)∗B)∗B, …}, with one basis element associated to each rooted full binary tree (i.e., each node has degree 0 or 2). Because of the A in (7), the order of an element with n Bs is 2n−1." Also: in the Euclidean case T = ½ p·p, "Φ_{T,V}(U₁∗U₂) = {V₂,{V₁,T}} = ∇V₁(q)·∇V₂(q)." — [arXiv:1905.07554 §2.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.07554)

**Proof technique:** "We construct L_P(A,B) by successively applying a Lazard factorization to eliminate the elements of degree 0 … L(Y₁ ∪ Y₂) ≅ L(Y₂) ⊕ L(∪_{n≥0}[Y₂ⁿ Y₁])." Basis: B = ∪_{n≥1} B_n with B₁ = {A,B} and for n ≥ 2, "B_n = {U ∈ H_n : U = [U₁,U₂], U₁,U₂ ∈ B, degree(U₂) > 0}" where H is a generalized Hall basis ordered first by degree then by order. — [arXiv:1905.07554 §2.2–2.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.07554)

### Inferences
- The imposed relations are "all brackets of degree-0 elements vanish", a strictly larger ideal than the one generated by [B,[B,[B,A]]] alone: e.g. [[B,[B,A]],[B,[B,[B,A]]]]-type elements (Table 1 lists [[B,[B,A]],[B,A]] as degree 0 and order 5 — that one is in B since [B,A] has degree 1, but [B,[B,[B,A]]] of order 4 is the first greyed element, and [[B,[B,[B,A]]],[B,A]] at order 6 is a further element in I not generated solely by brackets with [B,[B,[B,A]]] inside—consistent with the 2003 paper's remark "[BBBA] is not the only generator of the ideal ker ψ").
- From Table 1 and the definition B_n = {[U₁,U₂] : degree(U₂) > 0}, the order-4 count is 2 (not 3): the surviving order-4 elements are [[B,[B,A]],A] and [A,[[B,A],A]]; [B,[B,[B,A]]] is killed. This matches dim L⁴_P = 2 in Table 5 and d₄ = 2 in BCM2024 Table 2.1.
- The x_n column of Table 5 (1,1,1,2,3,6,11,23,46,98 for n = 1,3,…,19) coincides with the Wedderburn–Etherington numbers (rooted full binary trees, OEIS A001190), as required by Theorem 1; the 2003 paper had 12 (not 11) at order 13 — see Key Question 2.
- The printed "L^{min(n+m−1,0)}" in Definition 3 is almost certainly a typo for max(n+m−1,0) (degrees are ≥ 0 and [L²,L²] ⊆ L³); I flag it but did not alter the quotation.

## Key Question 2 — McLachlan & Ryland 2003 (arXiv:math-ph/0210030): dimension sequence c_n and grading

### Takeaway
Same definition (free Lie algebra in class P with degree(A) = 2, degree(B) = 0; grading by order has order(A) = order(B) = 1). Their Table 2 gives dim(L_P)ⁿ(A,B) for n = 1..40: 2,1,2,2,4,5,10,14,25,39,69,110,194,321,557,941,1638,2798,4878,8412,14692,25519,44683,77993,136928,240013,422360,742801,1310121,2310451,4083436,7218252,12781038,22638741,40152860,71247291,126559227,224917313,400080000,711997958 — agreeing with MM2019 for n ≤ 12 and larger from n = 13 on (194 vs 193). Entropy reported as 1.82542377420108…, versus 1.82491116… in 2019.

### Cited Findings

**Bibliographic.** "The algebraic entropy of classical mechanics", Robert I. McLachlan and Brett Ryland, arXiv:math-ph/0210030v1, 15 Oct 2002; published J. Math. Phys. 44 (2003) 3071–3087 (per MM2019 ref. [13]). — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/0210030)

**Abstract (verbatim excerpt):** "We describe the 'Lie algebra of classical mechanics', modelled on the Lie algebra generated by kinetic and potential energy of a simple mechanical system with respect to the canonical Poisson bracket. It is a polynomially graded Lie algebra, a class we introduce. We describe these Lie algebras, give an algorithm to calculate the dimensions c_n of the homogeneous subspaces of the Lie algebra of classical mechanics, and determine the value of its entropy lim_{n→∞} c_n^{1/n}. It is 1.82542377420108…, a fundamental constant associat[ed to classical mechanics]." — [arXiv:math-ph/0210030](https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/0210030)

**Witt's formula as quoted (Eq. 3):** "dim Lⁿ(A,B) = (1/n) Σ_{d|n} μ(d) 2^{n/d} … Notice that in this case dim Lⁿ(A,B) ∼ 2ⁿ/n; the dimensions grow exponentially with n. The base (2 in this case) of the exponent is called the entropy of L(A,B). In general, the entropy of a graded vector space ⊕ L_n is lim sup_{n→∞}(dim L_n)^{1/n}." — [arXiv:math-ph/0210030 §1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math-ph/0210030)

**Grading (Definitions 1–3, verbatim):** Definition 1 identical to MM2019 Def. 1 (eq. numbered (7) here): "[L_n,L_m] ⊆ L_{n+m−1} if n > 0 or m > 0; and [L_0,L_0] = 0 … We call the grading of L its grading by degree." "For example, the Lie algebra generated by kinetic and potential energy is of class P, where the grading is by total degree in p." Definition 2: "F is called a free Lie algebra in the class P, freely generated by the set X, if for any Lie algebra R of class P, every mapping X → R can be extended to a unique homomorphism F → R. We write F = L_P(X). In addition to the grading by degree, L_P(X) also carries the standard grading which we call the grading by order, generated by order(X) = 1 for all generators X ∈ X and order([Y,Z]) = order(Y)+order(Z). (The term order is chosen here because it corresponds to order in the sense of numerical integrators …)". Definition 3: "The Lie algebra L_P(A,B), free in the class P, where A has degree 2 and B has degree 0, is called the Lie algebra of classical mechanics." — [arXiv:math-ph/0210030 §1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math-ph/0210030)

**Two easy cases with more generators (verbatim, §1):** "First, the Lie algebra with k generators of degree ≥ 1 which is free in the class P is just the standard free Lie algebra on k generators—the degrees can never decrease if the Lie algebra has no elements of degree 0. Second, the Lie algebra with generators X = {X₁,…,X_k} of degree 0 and generators Y = {Y₁,…,Y_l} of degree 1, free in the class P, is Y ⊕ ⊕_{n≥0}[YⁿX], and only contains elements of degree 0 and 1." — [arXiv:math-ph/0210030 §1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math-ph/0210030)

**The ideal is not generated by [BBBA] alone (verbatim, §2):** "we showed above (Eq. (4)) that [BBBA] ∈ ker ψ. This implies that [CBBBA] ∈ ker ψ for all C ∈ L(A,B). However, we will see below that [BBBA] is not the only generator of the ideal ker ψ." — [arXiv:math-ph/0210030 §2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math-ph/0210030)

**Three generators A,B,C mentioned only as a device (verbatim):** "suppose one wants to describe the Lie algebra with three generators A, B, C which is free in the class of Lie algebras with C = 0. ## Interpretation (my own reading; not from the sources)

1. **Framework mismatch for the 3-body pairwise algebra.** The McLachlan–Murua/McLachlan–Ryland object is the *two*-generator free Lie algebra in class P with generators homogeneous in p (degree 2 and degree 0). The pairwise Hamiltonians H_ij = ½p_i² + ½p_j² + V(|q_i−q_j|) are *not* p-homogeneous (each is a degree-2 plus a degree-0 piece), so the class-P grading and the quotient L(A,B)/⟨[L⁰,L⁰]⟩ do not apply to them directly. MR2003's remark that a class-P-free algebra on generators all of degree ≥ 1 is just the ordinary free Lie algebra is also not applicable, since H_ij contains a degree-0 part. Any writer comparing the 3,3,8,17,44,… sequence to these papers should say the papers provide the *method* (identities forced by p-structure, generalized Witt counting) rather than a directly comparable number.

2. **Where the first deficit appears.** In the two-generator T+V case the first identity {V,{V,{V,T}}} = 0 reduces order 4 from 3 to 2 (MM2019 Table 5; BCM Table 2.1 d₄ = 2). In the task's 3-generator per-length sequence the first deficit is also at length 4 (17 vs 18), and the deficits then grow (4, 13, 40, 238 at lengths 5–8). A natural candidate explanation to test (not asserted by any source) is that linear combinations of the H_ij reproduce T + V-type structure — e.g. H₁₂ + H₁₃ + H₂₃ = 2T + V_total — so that the universal identity {V,{V,{V,T}}} ≡ 0 and its descendants, applied to degree-0 pieces built from the generators, impose relations of exactly the type the two-generator papers classify. Verifying this would require an explicit computation; none of the fetched sources does it.

3. **2003 vs 2019 numbers.** If the writer quotes "the Lie algebra of classical mechanics" dimensions, the 2019 values (193, 320, 555, 938, 1630, 2786, 4852, 8370 for n = 13–20) and entropy 1.8249111600523655937… should be used; the 2003 values (194, 321, 557, 941, 1638, 2798, 4878, 8412; entropy 1.82542377420108…) are explicitly retracted by MM2019 ("in error starting at order 13"). The corrected count of degree-0 elements is the Wedderburn–Etherington sequence (A001190), matching Theorem 1's rooted-full-binary-tree description.

4. **A395423 vs per-length counts.** A395423 is a depth filtration (dim of L_n, cumulative over all bracket lengths ≤ 2ⁿ), while 3,3,8,17,44,103,272,572 is a bracket-length grading. The two are consistent in the obvious checks (a(0) = 3 = length-1 count; a(1) = 6 = 3 + 3; a(2) = 17 ≤ 3+3+8+17 = 31 since depth-2 only reaches a subset of length-≤4 brackets). Neither the per-length sequence nor any OEIS entry other than A395423 matches these numbers as of 2026-11-08.

5. **What BCM2024 does and does not supply.** It supplies the canonical counting argument (order conditions at order n = dim Lₙ of the free Lie algebra, Lyndon basis, Witt), the RKN quotient column d_n (= MM2019 column 3 for n ≤ 11), and near-integrable error-term counts as closed forms; it does not supply any three-part or pairwise-N-body counts. The three-part free counts in the literature come from Munthe-Kaas & Owren 1999 as cited by Laskar–Vaillant, and equal A027376.
